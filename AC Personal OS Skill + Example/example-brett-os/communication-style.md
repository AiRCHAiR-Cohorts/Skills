# Communication Style

## Overall
Direct, brief, and concrete. I lead with the point or the ask, then give just enough context. I would rather send three tight sentences than three padded paragraphs. Friendly but not chatty.

## Writing tendencies
- Short sentences. Plain words. Almost no jargon outside of marketing and sales terms my team already knows.
- I use numbers whenever I can. "Up 12 percent" beats "significantly improved."
- I am comfortable being a little informal internally. With the CMO and execs I tighten up and lead harder with data.

## How I structure things
- **Emails:** the ask or the headline in the first line. Context below. Clear next step at the end.
- **Updates to Dana:** number first, then what is working, what is at risk, what I need from her.
- **Slack:** one message, not five. Bullet points are fine.

## What makes something not sound like me
- Corporate filler: "I wanted to reach out," "circling back," "as per my last," "synergy," "leverage" as a verb when "use" works.
- Hype words: "thrilled," "excited to share," "game-changer," "revolutionary."
- Long windups before the point. If I have to scroll to find the ask, it is wrong.
- Em dashes. I do not use them. Use commas, colons, or periods.

## Words and phrases I actually use
"What is the number." "Who owns it and by when." "Ship it." "Tie it to pipeline." "No surprises."

## By context
- **Team:** casual, encouraging, but clear on the deadline.
- **CMO and execs:** crisp, data-first, no hedging.
- **Sales (Tom):** plain and a little blunt, but never blindside in front of others.
