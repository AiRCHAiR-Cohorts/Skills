# CWRF: AI Agent Adoption (AirChAiR)

Domain-specific guidance for applying Crawl-Walk-Run-Fly to helping companies adopt AI agents across their organization, using the Accountability Chart as the deployment map.

## Context

Most companies approach AI adoption backwards — they start with the technology ("what can AI do?") instead of the organization ("where do we need help?"). AirChAiR flips this by using the Accountability Chart as the map. Every seat on the chart has 5 roles. Some of those roles are candidates for AI augmentation or full AI agent deployment. The CWRF framework provides the pacing so companies don't try to fly before they can crawl.

The AI Flywheel Effect is real: Crawl builds fundamental comfort and an AI-positive culture, which creates confidence. Walk tackles more sophisticated applications with organizational buy-in. Run executes complex automation faster because you have experienced, AI-literate teams. Fly achieves outcomes that were never possible without the technology.

## Phase Definitions

### Crawl — AI Literacy and Opportunity Mapping
The leadership team builds foundational understanding of what AI agents can and can't do, mapped against their actual organizational structure.

**Key Activities:**
- Map the Accountability Chart with all seats and their 5 roles
- Identify which roles involve repetitive, data-heavy, or pattern-matching work (AI-ready)
- Identify which roles require judgment, relationship, creativity, or physical presence (human-essential)
- Build basic AI literacy across the leadership team — what is an AI agent, what can it do, what are the risks
- Pick 1-2 low-risk, high-visibility pilot use cases (the "quick wins" that build cultural comfort)
- Establish baseline metrics for the pilot areas (time spent, error rates, cost, throughput)

**Graduation Criteria:**
- The leadership team can articulate where AI fits on their Accountability Chart — not as a buzzword but with specific role-level mapping
- At least one pilot is running and producing measurable results
- The team has moved from fear/hype to informed curiosity
- Someone on the team has become the internal AI champion

**Common Pitfall:** Analysis paralysis. The Crawl phase can become a permanent study group if the team never picks a pilot and starts. Another pitfall: picking a pilot that's too ambitious (trying to automate the Integrator's judgment) instead of something concrete and measurable (automating weekly Scorecard data collection).

### Walk — Expanding AI Deployment with Feedback Loops
Multiple AI agents are operating in defined roles, with human oversight and continuous iteration.

**Key Activities:**
- Expand from pilot to 3-5 AI agent deployments across different functions
- Build feedback loops — how are the agents performing? What's breaking? What's surprising?
- Train team members to work alongside AI agents (the human+AI workflow)
- Begin documenting AI-augmented Core Processes
- Establish governance — who owns each AI agent, how are they monitored, what are the escalation paths
- Address the human side — people's fear of being replaced, retrained, or made irrelevant

**Graduation Criteria:**
- Multiple AI agents are operating with minimal daily oversight
- Team members can articulate what the AI agents do, what they don't do, and when to intervene
- At least one process is measurably faster/cheaper/better with AI than without
- The cultural conversation has shifted from "will AI take my job?" to "how does AI help me do my job better?"
- Governance is documented and followed

**Common Pitfall:** Deploying AI agents without clear ownership on the Accountability Chart. An AI agent without a human owner becomes a black box that nobody trusts and nobody maintains. Every AI agent needs a seat (or a role within a seat) on the chart.

### Run — AI-Augmented Operations at Scale
AI agents are a normal part of how the company operates. The Accountability Chart reflects both human and AI roles.

**Key Activities:**
- AI agents handle significant portions of defined roles across multiple departments
- The Accountability Chart has been updated to reflect AI-augmented seats
- Cross-functional AI coordination is working (agents in different departments share data and insights)
- The company can onboard new AI capabilities without Duke or an external consultant
- Cost savings, speed improvements, and quality gains are tracked and compounding
- The company's competitive position is measurably stronger because of AI integration

**Graduation Criteria:**
- Removing the AI agents would cause significant operational disruption (they're load-bearing, not decorative)
- The team is proactively identifying new areas for AI deployment without prompting
- AI governance is routine, not heroic — it's baked into the L10 Meeting and Scorecard
- New hires are onboarded into AI-augmented workflows as a normal part of training

**Common Pitfall:** Over-reliance without redundancy. If an AI agent fails, does the team know how to do the work manually? Run doesn't mean "humans can't do this anymore" — it means "humans choose to let AI do this because it's better."

### Fly — Capabilities That Were Never Possible Before
The company achieves outcomes that have no human-only equivalent. This is the qualitative leap.

**Examples:**
- AI agents across the entire Accountability Chart collaborate to optimize workflows that span every department — a level of cross-functional coordination that was impossible with human-only teams
- The company's Scorecard is not just tracked but predicted — AI models forecast where measurables are heading and recommend interventions before numbers go off track
- Customer interactions are personalized at a depth and speed that no human team could achieve, creating competitive moats
- The Accountability Chart itself becomes dynamic — AI recommends structural changes based on workload patterns, skill gaps, and market conditions
- The company's Core Processes are continuously optimized by AI agents that identify bottlenecks, test improvements, and roll out changes automatically (with human approval)
- M&A due diligence is AI-augmented to evaluate acquisition targets in days instead of months

**The Fly Vision for AirChAiR:** A company where the Accountability Chart is not just a snapshot of who does what — it's a living system where humans and AI agents collaborate in real-time, with AI handling the work that benefits from speed, scale, and pattern recognition, and humans handling the work that requires judgment, relationship, creativity, and courage. The company doesn't just run well. It runs in ways that weren't imaginable before.
