# CWRF: General Organizational Change

Domain-specific guidance for applying Crawl-Walk-Run-Fly to any organizational transformation that doesn't fit neatly into EOS rollout, AI adoption, or exit readiness — new market entry, M&A integration, culture shifts, technology platform migrations, restructuring, and other change initiatives.

## Context

This is the most flexible domain. The CWRF framework provides universal pacing logic, but the specific activities, graduation criteria, and pitfalls must be adapted to the particular change initiative. Claude should use the diagnostic questions to understand the situation before generating a roadmap.

The throughline from Duke's vocational framework still applies: organizational change is fundamentally about helping people face what they've been avoiding. Every transformation has a technical dimension (what changes) and an emotional dimension (what people fear about the change). CWRF addresses both by pacing the technical work to match the emotional readiness of the team.

## Diagnostic Questions Before Building a Roadmap

Before generating a CWRF roadmap for general organizational change, Claude should understand:

1. **What is the change?** (Be specific — "digital transformation" is not specific enough. "Migrating from Salesforce to HubSpot while redefining our sales process" is.)
2. **Who is affected?** (Leadership team only? Entire organization? Specific departments? Customers?)
3. **What's driving the change?** (Pain, opportunity, external pressure, leadership vision?)
4. **What's the timeline?** (Is there an external deadline, or is this self-paced?)
5. **What has been tried before?** (Previous change attempts, what worked, what failed, what was avoided)
6. **What are people afraid of?** (Job loss, incompetence, loss of status, irrelevance, more work)
7. **Who are the champions and who are the resistors?** (Names and seats on the Accountability Chart if using EOS)
8. **What does success look like?** (Measurable outcomes, not just feelings)

## Phase Definitions (General Template)

### Crawl — Understand the Current State and Build the Case
Before changing anything, understand what you're changing and why — and make sure the people affected understand too.

**Key Activities:**
- Assess current state honestly (what's working, what's broken, what's being avoided)
- Define the desired future state in specific, measurable terms
- Identify stakeholders and their emotional posture toward the change
- Build the case for change — not just the business case but the human case (why this matters to the people doing the work)
- Pick 1-2 early wins that demonstrate the change is possible and beneficial
- Establish baseline metrics

**Graduation Criteria:**
- The people affected can explain why the change is happening in their own words
- At least one early win has been achieved and celebrated
- Resistance has been surfaced and addressed (not suppressed)
- The leadership team is aligned on the change — no silent dissenters

**Common Pitfall:** Announcing the change and assuming comprehension equals buy-in. Crawl is about understanding, not just informing.

### Walk — Implement with Coaching and Iteration
The new way of working is being practiced, with support, in real conditions.

**Key Activities:**
- Implement the change in defined areas (departments, teams, or processes)
- Provide coaching and support — don't just train and leave
- Collect feedback actively and iterate based on what's learned
- Address resistance as it surfaces (it will intensify in Walk because the change is now real, not theoretical)
- Document what's working and what's not — build the playbook as you go
- Celebrate milestones to build momentum

**Graduation Criteria:**
- The new way of working is being executed without daily coaching
- People are iterating and improving on their own, not waiting for instructions
- Measurables are trending positive (even if not yet at target)
- The "old way" is no longer the default — people have to actively choose to revert, and they don't want to
- Internal champions are emerging organically

**Common Pitfall:** Moving to Run too quickly because the leadership team is impatient. Walk is where the muscle memory gets built. Rushing past it means the organization snaps back to old habits under stress.

### Run — The New Normal
The change is embedded. It's how things are done. Execution is confident and consistent.

**Key Activities:**
- The new processes/tools/structures operate at full speed with full team participation
- Performance metrics are meeting or exceeding targets
- The change is self-sustaining — it doesn't require champion energy to maintain
- New people are onboarded into the new way of working as the default
- Continuous improvement is happening within the new framework

**Graduation Criteria:**
- An observer who wasn't present during the change would assume "this is how it's always been done"
- The team can handle disruptions (personnel changes, market shifts, technology issues) without reverting
- The original drivers for the change have been resolved or are measurably improving
- Leadership attention has shifted from managing the change to leveraging its benefits

**Common Pitfall:** Declaring victory too early. Run is not "we survived the transition." Run is "the transition is complete and the new state is performing as promised." The gap between those two is where many change initiatives die quietly.

### Fly — Beyond the Original Vision
The change enables capabilities and outcomes that weren't part of the original plan — because they weren't possible before.

**How to identify Fly potential:**
- Look for intersections between the change and emerging technology (especially AI)
- Ask: "What would we do if we could process information 100x faster? Serve 10x more customers? Predict outcomes instead of reacting to them?"
- Look for compounding effects — where does this change, combined with other capabilities, create something entirely new?

**The Fly Vision should be:**
- Specific to this organization and this change (not generic "AI will help")
- Genuinely impossible without the preceding phases (Crawl/Walk/Run were necessary preconditions)
- Exciting enough to justify the discomfort of the journey
- Grounded in real technology and real business logic (not science fiction)

**Common Pitfall:** Treating Fly as mandatory. Not every change initiative needs a Fly phase. Some changes are about getting from dysfunction to function — and Run is a worthy destination. Fly is most relevant when the change involves technology adoption, new market creation, or fundamental business model evolution.
