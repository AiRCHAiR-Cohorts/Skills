# CWRF: EOS Tool Rollout to Departments

Domain-specific guidance for applying Crawl-Walk-Run-Fly to rolling out EOS foundational tools (Accountability Chart, Rocks, L10 Meetings, Scorecard, V/TO) from the leadership team to the rest of the organization.

## Context

The leadership team has been running on EOS tools for some time. Now the question is: how do we get the rest of the company operating with the same discipline? This is where most EOS implementations either compound or stall. The rollout is not just installing tools — it's transferring ownership of a new way of working to people who weren't in the room when the decisions were made.

Duke's rollout framework (from his session materials) provides the content. CWRF provides the pacing and graduation logic.

## Phase Definitions

### Crawl — Introduce and Educate
The leadership team presents EOS to the broader organization. The goal is comprehension, not execution.

**Key Activities:**
- Deliver the rollout presentation (Overview of EOS, V/TO walkthrough, Core Values Speech, Accountability Chart, Rocks explanation, L10 Meeting agenda, Scorecard overview)
- Answer questions honestly — "any thoughts, questions, concerns, good ideas"
- Let people sit with it. Don't rush to implementation.
- Identify departmental champions who show natural curiosity

**Graduation Criteria:**
- Every employee can explain what EOS is and why the company is using it in their own words
- Every employee can name the company's Core Values and Core Focus
- Every employee knows where they sit on the Accountability Chart and can name their 5 roles
- Questions have shifted from "what is this?" to "how do we use it?"

**Common Pitfall:** The leadership team treats the rollout presentation as a one-time event rather than the beginning of a learning process. One meeting is not Crawl — it's the first step of Crawl.

### Walk — Practice the Tools with Coaching
Departments begin using EOS tools in their own context, with coaching from the leadership team (and Duke where needed).

**Key Activities:**
- Departments create their own Rocks (aligned to company Rocks)
- Departments establish their own L10 Meeting rhythm
- Departments build departmental Scorecards with their own measurables
- Leadership team members coach their departments through early iterations
- Issues surface — this is expected and healthy

**Graduation Criteria:**
- Departments are running L10 Meetings on their own without leadership team members facilitating
- Departmental Rocks are being completed at 80%+ rate
- Departmental Scorecards are being maintained weekly without reminders
- Issues are being IDS'd at the departmental level (not all escalated to leadership team)
- The phrase "that's a Rock" or "let's IDS that" enters the department's natural vocabulary

**Common Pitfall:** Leadership team members micromanaging departmental L10s instead of coaching and releasing. The Walk phase requires the LT to let go — which triggers their own fear of losing control.

### Run — Independent Execution with Accountability
Departments are running EOS tools with proficiency and discipline. The system is self-sustaining.

**Key Activities:**
- Departmental L10s run on time, on agenda, rated 8+ consistently
- Cross-departmental issues are identified and escalated appropriately
- Department leaders are coaching their own teams on EOS tools
- The Scorecard drives decisions, not just reports
- Process documentation (Core Processes) is being followed and improved by the departments themselves

**Graduation Criteria:**
- Duke (or the Integrator) could step out for a quarter and the departments would maintain EOS discipline
- New hires are onboarded into EOS tools by their department, not by the leadership team
- The company's Organizational Checkup score is consistently improving
- Departmental issues rarely surprise the leadership team — the system surfaces them early

**Common Pitfall:** Complacency. "We've got this" leads to skipping L10 agenda items, letting Scorecards slide, or treating Rocks as suggestions. Run requires sustained discipline, not just initial enthusiasm.

### Fly — The Organization Becomes Predictive and Self-Improving
Technology and organizational maturity combine to create capabilities that weren't possible with human-only EOS execution.

**Examples:**
- AI agents analyze patterns across all departmental L10 Issues Lists and surface systemic themes before the leadership team's quarterly session — the Issues List becomes predictive
- Scorecard data is automatically collected, trend-analyzed, and anomaly-flagged — humans focus on interpretation, not data entry
- Rock completion patterns are analyzed to identify which departments consistently over-commit or under-deliver, enabling proactive coaching
- A new hire's onboarding into EOS is partially AI-tutored (using the CWRF mastery bot approach), freeing department leaders' time
- Cross-departmental process handoffs are monitored by AI agents that flag breakdowns before they become issues

**The Fly Vision for EOS Rollout:** An organization where the operating system is not just running — it's learning. The EOS tools don't just track the business; they actively improve it by surfacing insights no human could see across the full complexity of the organization.
