# CWRF: Exit Readiness

Domain-specific guidance for applying Crawl-Walk-Run-Fly to a privately-held company building toward exit, transition, or owner independence — whether the destination is a sale, a generational handoff, an ESOP, or simply the freedom of running a self-managing business.

## The Core Insight

Exit readiness is not an event you trigger 18 months before sale. It is a state of organizational maturity that compounds over years. The same disciplines that make a company run well day-to-day (clear vision, right people in right seats, documented processes, data-driven decisions, disciplined execution) are exactly what make a company attractive to acquirers, investable for PE firms, and transferable to next-generation leadership.

Most owners don't think about exit readiness until they're 2-3 years from wanting to sell — by which time it's often too late to maximize value. CWRF gives owners a framework for progressive maturation that starts early and compounds over time.

## The Four Phases

### Crawl — Owner-Dependent Operation

The business runs because the owner runs it. Most knowledge lives in the owner's head. Customer relationships, vendor relationships, key processes, and strategic thinking all route through the founder.

**What's true here:**
- Revenue exists, but it's tied to the owner's effort and presence
- Key processes are tribal knowledge, not documented
- Financials may be clean or messy, but they're not "investor-ready"
- The owner is the bottleneck for most decisions
- If the owner took 30 days off, the business would visibly suffer

**Graduation criteria (Crawl → Walk):**
- Clean, accrual-basis financials with at least 12 months of clean data
- A documented organizational structure showing functions and roles (not just people)
- Identification of the 3-5 processes that, if undocumented, would be unrecoverable if the owner left
- Honest assessment of owner-dependency risk in customer and vendor relationships

**Common pitfall:** Skipping the financials cleanup because "we know what we make." Acquirers and lenders cannot underwrite tribal knowledge. The numbers must be defensible.

### Walk — Process-Documented, Owner-Reduced

The business has documented systems for the work that matters most. The owner is still essential but increasingly strategic rather than operational. A leadership team is forming or formed.

**What's true here:**
- Core processes are documented and followed by the team, not just the owner
- A leadership team exists with clear roles and accountability
- The owner spends measurably less time on operational firefighting
- Financials are clean enough that an outside party could understand the business
- Customer concentration risk and key-person risk are identified and being addressed

**Graduation criteria (Walk → Run):**
- The leadership team can run a 90-day execution cycle without the owner driving every decision
- Process documentation is current (within 12 months) and being maintained
- The business has run a "what if the owner disappeared for 90 days" tabletop exercise honestly
- A baseline business valuation has been done — even if just internal

**Common pitfall:** Documenting processes that nobody follows. Documentation without enforcement is theater. The Walk graduation test is whether the team actually uses the documentation.

### Run — Self-Managing with Owner as Capital Allocator

The business runs without the owner's daily involvement. The owner's role shifts to strategy, capital allocation, and high-leverage relationships. The leadership team owns operations.

**What's true here:**
- The owner could disappear for 90 days and the business would continue to perform
- A capable second-in-command (Integrator, COO, President) is in place
- Financials are auditable, predictable, and supportive of an exit narrative
- Customer concentration and key-person risks are mitigated structurally
- The business has been valued by an outside party (broker, investment banker, valuation firm)
- A formal exit timeline and target outcome exist (even if 5+ years out)

**Graduation criteria (Run → Fly):**
- The business has the operational maturity to be acquired today, even if the owner doesn't want to sell
- Multiple potential paths to exit have been mapped (strategic sale, PE recap, ESOP, family transition)
- The owner has a clear post-exit identity and life plan that doesn't depend on the business

**Common pitfall:** Confusing Run with the finish line. Run is the platform from which Fly becomes possible — not the destination.

### Fly — Exit-Irresistible Asset

The company has reached a state where it's not just exit-ready, it's exit-irresistible. The acquirer isn't buying a business that can survive without the founder. They're buying a business that gets *better* without the founder, because the intelligence is in the system, not the person.

**What's possible here:**
- AI-augmented operating systems that compound the leadership team's leverage
- Self-improving processes — the company gets sharper without the owner's intervention
- Predictable, defensible cash flow that supports premium multiples
- A leadership team that the acquirer wants to keep, not replace
- The owner walks away with pride, not grief — having built something that transcends them

**The qualitative leap:** Crawl/Walk/Run = humans getting progressively better at running a business. Fly = humans + technology + systems doing things that were never on the menu before. The company is no longer dependent on any single person, including the next CEO.

## Diagnosing Phase: Quick Questions

- Does the business have clean, accrual-basis financials going back 24+ months? (If no → Crawl)
- Could the owner take a true 30-day vacation without checking in daily? (If no → Crawl or Walk)
- Is there a leadership team that owns operational decisions? (If no → Crawl or Walk)
- Has an outside party valued the business in the last 24 months? (If no → likely Walk)
- Could the owner disappear for 90 days and the business would perform without them? (If yes → Run)
- Is the company demonstrably better at things it could never do before AI/automation existed? (If yes → entering Fly)

## Common Pitfalls Across All Phases

- **Treating exit readiness as a transaction**, not a state. The state takes years to build; the transaction takes months.
- **Confusing revenue with value.** A $20M business with 70% owner-dependency is worth less than a $10M business that runs without the owner.
- **Optimizing for the wrong buyer.** Strategic acquirers, PE firms, ESOPs, and family transitions all reward different attributes. Knowing the destination shapes the path.
- **Owner identity collapse.** The owner who hasn't built a post-exit life often sabotages the exit unconsciously. Vocational and identity work matters as much as financial work.
